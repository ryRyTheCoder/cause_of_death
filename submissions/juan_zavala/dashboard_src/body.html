Read this before drawing conclusions

"Unknown" age is mostly babies

{{unknown_pct}} of burials have no age written. {{unk_infant_pct}} of those are entries like "Infant", "Inft." or "Still born", or have an infant cause such as teething. The assignment's rule puts them in "unknown", which is why "child" looks rare.

One cemetery, not the whole city

These are burials in the City Cemetery, not deaths in Nashville. Burials fall off after 1870, most likely because newer cemeteries took over (general knowledge, not shown in the ledger), so the decline is not evidence of falling mortality.

1869 is probably incomplete

It has only {{n_1869}} rows, against {{n_1868}} in 1868 and {{n_1870}} in 1870. Infant entries fall to {{inf_1869_pct}} of the year's burials, against {{inf_1868_pct}} in 1868, and it is the only year that uses the race code "D". Treat the dip as a likely gap in the records.

{{n_dup}} duplicates removed

For 1879–1881, some burials were written in both a yearly book and a lettered book (6A…6Z). The lettered-book copies are left out.

Clerks' records, not certificates

Any single entry can be wrong. The ledger gives former President Polk's age as {{polk_age}}, while standard accounts give 53 (not checked here). Read the patterns, not individual rows.

Context needs checking

The notebook's historical context (the cholera years, the Union occupation of Nashville) comes from general knowledge and was not checked against sources.
